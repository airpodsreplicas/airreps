# Do Fake AirPods Work With New iPhones?

Short answer: yes. A replica's compatibility with a new iPhone has far more to do with the version you bought than with the phone in your pocket. Apple's phones all speak the same Bluetooth and the same popup language, so a working pair keeps working when you upgrade. The differences people actually run into are version differences — chipset, firmware, and feature list — not phone differences.

This is the plain version of what does and doesn't change when the phone is new and the buds are fake.

## The iOS popup still works

The pairing popup is the first thing people worry about, and it's the least of your problems. Replicas show it almost instantly, and the speed depends on the seller and batch rather than the phone — in some cases it appears *faster* than on genuine AirPods. Our [connectivity notes](/introduction/connectivity) cover how this behaves, including the rare case where a fresh pair won't connect on the first try and needs a reset.

If the popup shows up, iOS is treating the buds as a headset. That's the part the phone handles, and it's the same on the newest model as on an older one.

## Naming and tap controls are set up through iOS

Name changing, GPS location display, and configurable tap controls are all Apple-software features, and all of them work on iPhone. Every current model supports name changing, GPS, and changing tap controls through iOS. That's true across the lineup — see the [general version info](/version-info/general) for the shared list.

The practical note here is that these settings live on the buds once you make them, so they carry over if you later move the pair to another device. On iPhone there's nothing special to do: connect, open Settings, change what you want.

## What actually varies: the version, not the phone

Here's where the real compatibility questions live. Three things genuinely differ between versions, and none of them are about how new your iPhone is.

- **iCloud Connect is Huilian-only.** Automatic switching between Apple devices only works on Huilian-based models — for example the AirPods Pro 2 V5.4 and the AirPods Pro 3 V6 Huilian. Other chipsets connect to multiple devices at once, but you switch manually. Our [connectivity page](/introduction/connectivity) spells this out.
- **Live Translation and the newer feature set are on the current Pro 3 versions.** The [AirPods Pro 3 page](/version-info/airpods-pro-3) lists Live Translation on the V6 (Huilian only), the V7, and the SE, alongside features like the iOS 26 support listing, the About page, heart rate sensing, and head gesture controls on the top versions. Older models simply don't have these, regardless of the phone.
- **Older low-tier clones with Jieli chipsets may not connect to the community apps at all.** The [general FAQ](/version-info/general) and [useful apps](/useful-apps) page are both direct about this: if none of the firmware apps detect your pair, it's a low-tier clone. That's a chipset limitation, not an iPhone one.

In other words, if a feature is missing on your reps, the phone upgrade won't add it back. The version you bought is the ceiling.

## Match the version to your phone's iOS generation

The sensible buying rule is to pick a version whose feature list matches the iOS generation you're actually running, then confirm it on the version info page rather than trusting a seller's claim. Listings describe clones with vague names and unreliable chip photos; the community numbering exists precisely because those claims don't hold up. Compare versions on the [version info](/version-info/general) page, ask the [quiz](/quiz) for a recommendation, and buy from a [trusted seller](/links/info).

If you're on an older version of iOS, you don't need the newest replica to match — a proven previous-generation model often makes more sense than an early release with rough edges. Match the product to your phone's software generation, not to the marketing.

## Do iOS updates break replicas?

Usually not — but it does happen, and it's worth knowing the pattern. The great majority of versions that worked before an iOS update keep working after it. When something does break, it's almost always a **major** iOS release rather than a point update, and it hits **older** versions whose firmware predates the change. The iOS 27 rollout is the most recent example the community ran into.

The good news is how quickly it resolves. When a major iOS release breaks a version, the manufacturer typically follows with a firmware update fairly quickly, and updating the replica restores the functionality — it's a temporary incompatibility, not a dead product. Updating goes through the community [firmware apps](/useful-apps), which are mostly Android-only, so it's worth knowing that before you buy if you don't keep an Android device around. In the worst case, a version that never gets a fix is usually an older or lower-tier model.

Two practical notes before you hit "Update" on a major release:

- Check the [AirReps Discord](https://airreps.link/discord) first — that's where breakage gets reported, on day one, by people running new firmware and new iOS together.
- This is a rare occurrence, and it's a decent argument for staying on a current version rather than a two-year-old one that may no longer receive firmware updates.

Nothing here is a guarantee of support on any future iOS release — check current reports for your specific version.

## FAQ

::: details Will fake AirPods work with an iPhone 16?

Yes. Nothing about the phone model blocks a working replica. The pairing popup still appears, and naming and tap controls still work through iOS. If your pair has an issue on a new iPhone, the cause is almost always the version or firmware, not the phone.

:::

::: details Do replica AirPods work with iOS 26?

Many current versions list iOS 26 support — the Pro 3 V5.5, V6, and V7 all do, and the SE lists a newer iOS generation still. Older and lower-tier clones are the ones that struggle. Check the [AirPods Pro 3 page](/version-info/airpods-pro-3) and the [general comparison](/version-info/general) for your exact version rather than assuming.

:::

::: details Why won't my replica connect to the firmware apps?

Either the app doesn't match your chipset or your pair uses a low-end Jieli chipset that no community app supports. Work through the apps on the [useful apps](/useful-apps) page one at a time — if none connect, it's a low-tier clone. This has nothing to do with your iPhone.

:::

::: details Will a new iOS update break my replicas?

Occasionally — but it's rare and usually short-lived. Breakage tends to appear with major iOS releases and hits older versions whose firmware predates the change; the iOS 27 rollout was the most recent case. Manufacturers usually ship a firmware update soon after, and updating the replica through the [firmware apps](/useful-apps) restores the functionality it had before. Check the [Discord](https://airreps.link/discord) before a major update if you want to know whether your version is affected.

:::
