# Do AirPods Replicas Work With Android?

Yes. AirPods replicas pair with Android like any Bluetooth earbuds — connect, play music, take calls, adjust volume. The iOS-only parts of the experience are the ones tied to Apple software, and a few of them have workarounds. There's even one area where Android users get the *better* deal: the firmware apps. Here's the full picture.

## Pairing: no popup, same result

The famous pairing popup is an iPhone feature — on Android you pair from the Bluetooth menu like any other headset. That's a cosmetic difference, not a capability one: after pairing, everything else behaves like normal earbuds. (If you also own an iPhone, you can use it for the one-tap setup and then switch the buds to your Android.)

## What works on Android

- **Core playback** — music, calls, volume, and track controls all work.
- **ANC and transparency** — these are chip features, not Apple features, so they work the same on Android.
- **Range and latency** — the same ~10 meter range as genuine AirPods, with latency often on par or better.
- **Multi-point connections** — most replicas connect to several devices at once, which is handy if you bounce between a phone and a laptop.

## What needs an iPhone

A few conveniences are Apple-software features and behave differently off iOS:

- **Name changing and tap-control configuration** are done through iOS. The trick from our [features guide](/introduction/features): borrow an iPhone once, make your settings changes there, and they carry over to the buds themselves — most of the time it sticks.
- **GPS location display** in Apple's services is iOS-only.
- **iCloud Connect multi-device switching** (automatic device switching between Apple devices) only applies to Apple hardware, and only on Huilian models — on Android you switch devices manually like with any earbuds.

None of these affect day-to-day Android use; they're setup conveniences and Apple-ecosystem extras.

## The Android advantage: firmware apps

Here's the twist — **most of the community's firmware apps are Android-only**. These apps update your replicas' firmware (which can add features like Spatial Audio or improve stability) and tune the EQ, and they're how you identify which chip your pair uses in the first place.

The main ones — FlyCC, CloudCC, BullSuper, StarFun, KKX and more — are catalogued with download links and chipset support tables on our [useful apps](/useful-apps) page. Two things to know before installing:

1. They're not on the Play Store. Enable **"Install from unknown sources"** in your security settings first.
2. Match the app to your chip. Not sure which chip you have? The apps themselves are the test — try them one by one and see which connects, or read the [version info](/version-info/general) page first.

## FAQ

::: details Do fake AirPods connect to Android?
Yes — replicas pair with Android like any Bluetooth earbuds. The only thing missing is Apple's iOS pairing popup, which is an iPhone feature that genuine AirPods don't have on Android either.
:::

::: details Can you change the name of fake AirPods on Android?
Not directly — renaming and tap-control settings are done through iOS. The workaround is to make the changes once on any iPhone; they save to the earbuds and carry over to your Android. Some chipsets also have their own Android apps that can do more.
:::

::: details Why won't my replica connect to the firmware apps?
Either the app doesn't match your chip, or your replicas use a low-end Jieli chipset that no community app supports. Work through the apps on the [useful apps](/useful-apps) page one at a time — if none connect, you have a low-tier clone.
:::

::: details Do replica AirPods sound worse on Android?
No. Sound quality comes from the driver and chip in the earbuds, not the phone. If anything, Android users can tune the sound further, since the EQ-capable firmware apps are mostly Android-only.
:::
